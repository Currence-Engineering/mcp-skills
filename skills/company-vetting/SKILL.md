# Company Vetting

Separate real traction from pitch-deck claims, and give the user the questions to ask. Requires the Currence MCP.

## Steps
1. Identify the company and its technology. If no company is named, vet the technology and say so.
2. Benchmark the technology: read the Currence tech profile and sector compass. Extract the readiness score, the largest scale running today, cost and performance benchmarks, and the main barriers. If the user quoted a claim (such as a TRL), compare it directly with the Currence score.
3. Check the record: funding history and investors, deals, projects (with status and scale), contracts and offtakes, and market events from the last 12 months.
4. Compare with peers: choose the 3 to 5 closest companies by sub-sector, stage and geography. Compare stage, total raised, deployments and contracts in one table.
5. Call out the gaps: every claim that the Currence record does not support becomes a question.
6. Write the questions in four groups, 3 to 5 each:
   - **Technology**: readiness and performance against the benchmark.
   - **Commercial**: customers, offtake, pipeline.
   - **Capital**: the capital stack and who follows on.
   - **Execution**: team, permitting, supply chain.
7. List the KPIs to request, with the Currence benchmark where one exists, and the red flags specific to this technology.
8. Link the company, peers, tech profile and compass to the Currence platform.

## Tips
- For a technology you do not know, start from the tech profile. That is where the readiness scoring lives.
- Projects and contracts are stronger evidence than press releases. Say which one each point rests on.

## Limits
- Currence does not hold private company financials. Treat revenue, margins and unit economics as questions for the founder, not facts.
- Tech profiles are not yet linked to every company deploying them. If the link is missing, say so rather than guessing.

## Format
Lead with a verdict in one line: claims supported, partly supported, or unsupported, and the single biggest reason. Then the peer table, then the question checklist the user can take into the meeting.

# Thesis Builder

Turn a theme into a thesis that survives an investment committee. Requires the Currence MCP.

## Steps
1. State the thesis in one sentence. If the user gave a theme rather than a claim, write the claim for them and say so.
2. Break it into 3 to 5 drivers that must be true for the thesis to hold, for example cost falls below a threshold, policy creates demand, or a buyer group starts signing contracts.
3. Read Currence research first: the relevant sector compass, tech profiles and analyst research. Use them to test whether the drivers are the right ones.
4. For each driver, gather evidence for and against from Currence data: deals, projects, contracts, policies and market events. Rate each driver as supported, mixed or unsupported.
5. Map the players: leading companies by sub-category, with stage and total raised.
6. Map the capital: deals in the last 24 months, the most active investors by deal count, and any acquirers.
7. Flag the whitespace: sub-categories, geographies or buyer groups with demand signals but few credible players.
8. List the open questions that would change the verdict, and the signposts to watch.
9. Link every company, deal, project and report to the Currence platform.

## Tips
- For niche themes, search market events by keyword. Specialist topics are often tagged under a broader sector, so keyword search finds more.
- Evidence against is as useful as evidence for. Do not drop it to make the thesis look stronger.

## Limits
- Currence does not hold company revenue, valuation multiples (unless disclosed) or fund performance. Do not estimate them.
- Funding totals grouped by many sub-sectors in one query are not reliable yet. Pull sub-sectors one at a time.

## Format
Verdict first: investable now, investable with conditions, or too early, with the one reason that matters most. Then a drivers table (driver, evidence for, evidence against, rating), the player and capital map, whitespace, and open questions.
